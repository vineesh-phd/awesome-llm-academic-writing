# References

All 20 papers below were independently verified against their primary source (arXiv, DOI/Crossref, ACM DL, or JMIR) — title, authors, and year confirmed to match, not accepted on an AI tool's word.

## Survey Papers

- **Large Language Models for Automated Scholarly Paper Review: A Survey**
  Zhuang, Z., Chen, J., Xu, H., Jiang, Y., & Lin, J. (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.10326)
  Comprehensive survey of LLMs used specifically in automated manuscript review — relevant background on how LLM involvement in scholarly workflows is being systematized and evaluated.

- **How much are LLMs changing the language of academic papers after ChatGPT? A multi-database and full text analysis**
  (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09596)
  Large-scale analysis of over 2.4 million PubMed Central full-text articles (2021–2025) measuring the frequency and correlation of LLM-associated terms — direct evidence of the scale of the homogenization question.

- **Exploring the adoption of ChatGPT in academic publishing: insights and lessons for scientific writing**
  Homolak, J. (2023), *Croatian Medical Journal*, 64(3), 205–207
  [Paper (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.3325/cmj.2023.64.205)
  Early editorial highlighting the inconsistency of AI-detection tools on scientific abstracts, framing the verification challenge this repository's own citation audit addresses.

## Foundational Papers

- **A Comprehensive Overview of Large Language Models**
  Naveed, H., Khan, A.U., Qiu, S., Saqib, M., Anwar, S., Usman, M., Akhtar, N., Barnes, N., & Mian, A. (2023), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.06435)
  Widely-cited survey of LLM architectures and emergent capabilities underlying every downstream academic-writing application discussed in this repository.

## Stylistic & Lexical Impact Studies

- **ChatGPT as Linguistic Equalizer? Quantifying LLM-Driven Lexical Shifts in Academic Writing**
  Lin, D., Zhao, N., Tian, D., & Li, J. (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.12317)
  Causal (difference-in-differences) analysis of 2.8M OpenAlex articles showing ChatGPT raises lexical complexity for non-native-English authors — the core "linguistic equalizer" evidence.

- **Homogenization Effects of Large Language Models on Human Creative Ideation**
  Anderson, B.R., Shah, J.H., & Kreminski, M. (2024), *Creativity and Cognition (C&C '24)*
  [Paper (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1145/3635636.3656204)
  36-participant controlled study finding ChatGPT users produce less semantically distinct ideas than users of an alternative creativity-support tool — direct experimental evidence for mono-voice convergence.

- **Homogenizing effect of large language models (LLMs) on creative diversity: An empirical comparison of human and ChatGPT writing**
  Moon, K., Green, A.E., & Kushlev, K. (2025), *Computers in Human Behavior: Artificial Humans*, 6, 100207
  [Paper (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chbah.2025.100207)
  Direct empirical comparison of human vs. ChatGPT writing diversity, quantifying the homogenization effect this repository's paper centers on.

- **Does Writing with Language Models Reduce Content Diversity?**
  Padmakumar, V., & He, H. (2024), *ICLR 2024*
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05196)
  Controlled co-writing experiment showing InstructGPT (but not base GPT-3) significantly reduces lexical and content diversity — introduces the "algorithmic monoculture" framing.

- **The Homogenizing Effect of Large Language Models on Human Expression and Thought**
  Sourati, Z., Ziabari, A.S., & Dehghani, M. (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.01491)
  Broader empirical treatment of LLM-driven homogenization extending beyond writing style into human thought patterns generally.

- **Divergent LLM Adoption and Heterogeneous Convergence Paths in Research Writing**
  Cong, L.W., & Zhu, W. (2024), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13629)
  627,000-paper arXiv analysis showing convergence toward established researchers' style is uneven — most pronounced among early adopters, non-native speakers, and junior scholars.

- **Examining Linguistic Shifts in Academic Writing Before and After the Launch of ChatGPT: A Study on Preprint Papers**
  Bao, T., Zhao, Y., Mao, J., & Zhang, C. (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12218)
  823,798-abstract analysis finding Computer Science shows the most pronounced post-ChatGPT style shift and Mathematics the least — evidence of disciplinary variation in homogenization risk.

## Automated Writing & Literature Review Generation

- **Large Language Models for Automated Literature Review: An Evaluation of Reference Generation, Abstract Writing, and Review Composition**
  Tang, X., Duan, X., & Cai, Z.G. (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.13612)
  Introduces hallucination-rate and semantic-coverage metrics for LLM-generated literature reviews; source of the claim that even advanced models still fabricate references.

- **AutoSurvey: Large Language Models Can Automatically Write Surveys**
  Wang, Y., Guo, Q., Yao, W., et al. (2024), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10252)
  Concrete automated-survey-writing pipeline addressing context-window and evaluation-benchmark limitations in LLM-generated surveys.

- **InteractiveSurvey: An LLM-based Personalized and Interactive Survey Paper Generation System**
  (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.08762)
  RAG-based system letting users customize outline and reference categorization during generation — a working implementation, not just a method paper.

- **Evaluation Sheet for Deep Research: A Use Case for Academic Survey Writing**
  Azime, I.A., Belay, T.D., & Tonja, A.L. (2025), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01283)
  Proposes and applies an evaluation rubric comparing OpenAI's and Google's "deep research" agentic tools on academic survey generation.

## Ethics, Authorship & Integrity

- **ChatGPT for scientific paper writing—promises and perils**
  He, S., Yang, F., Zuo, J.-P., & Lin, Z.-M. (2023), *The Innovation*, 4(6), 100524
  [Paper (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xinn.2023.100524)
  Editorial framing LLM writing assistance as a "double-edged sword" — origin of the stylistic-stagnation and authorship-accountability concerns central to this repository's topic.

- **Do Language Models Know When They're Hallucinating References?**
  Agrawal, A., Suzgun, M., Mackey, L., & Kalai, A.T. (2024), *Findings of the Association for Computational Linguistics: EACL 2024*, pp. 912–928
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.18248)
  Proposes hallucinated references as a tractable "model organism" for studying LLM hallucination — foundational for the citation-integrity angle of this repository.

- **On the Detectability of ChatGPT Content: Benchmarking, Methodology, and Evaluation through the Lens of Academic Writing**
  Liu, Z., Yao, Z., Li, F., & Luo, B. (2024), *ACM CCS '24*
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05524)
  2.8M-sample benchmark (GPABench2) plus a detector (CheckGPT) built specifically for academic abstracts; shows detectors perform poorly on GPT-polished (vs. fully generated) text.

- **ChatGPT or academic scientist? Distinguishing authorship with over 99% accuracy using off-the-shelf machine learning tools**
  Desaire, H., Chua, A.E., Isom, M., Jarosova, R., & Hua, D. (2023), arXiv preprint
  [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.16352)
  Demonstrates high-accuracy authorship attribution using simple, interpretable stylometric features rather than deep detectors.

- **Influence of Topic Familiarity and Prompt Specificity on Citation Fabrication in Mental Health Research Using Large Language Models: Experimental Study**
  Linardon, J., Jarman, H.K., McClure, Z., Anderson, C., Liu, C., & Messer, M. (2025), *JMIR*
  [Paper (DOI)](https://doi.org/10.2196/80371)
  Shows citation-fabrication rates vary systematically with topic familiarity and prompt specificity — relevant to why fabrication rates may vary across the sub-topics an audit covers.
